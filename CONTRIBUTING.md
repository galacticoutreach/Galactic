# Contributing

Thanks for considering it. This page says what contributing to Galactic
Outreach means, and how to do it so your report is useful. If you are unsure
about any of it, email hello@galacticoutreach.com and ask.

## Proprietary, with private source

Galactic Outreach is **proprietary software**. The application's source code is
**private**: it is not in this repository and is not published anywhere, so
there is nothing to fork and no code pull request to open. This repository
holds the README, the banner and the [licence](./LICENSE), which grants no
right to use, copy or redistribute any part of the product.

That does not make contributions less useful. You use the product on your own
data, through your own email provider, against your own market. What you run
into there, we cannot find on our own.

## What you can contribute

- **Bug reports.** Something behaved wrongly. This is the most valuable thing
  you can send.
- **Feature ideas and feedback.** Something got in your way, or would work
  better another way.
- **Documentation corrections.** Anything in the
  [docs](https://galacticoutreach.com/docs/home) that is wrong, stale or
  confusing.
- **README fixes.** A typo, a broken link or an out-of-date line in this
  repository's README. These can be a pull request.
- **Security vulnerabilities.** These go a different, private route: see
  [SECURITY.md](./SECURITY.md). Never open a public issue for one.

## Where to send it

- **The Support screen inside the dashboard** is best for anything about your
  own account or campaign. It attaches your email address, your campaign and
  your browser automatically, and keeps a dated trail of what happens to your
  report. The reply comes by email.
- **A GitHub issue** in this repository, for bugs, ideas and documentation
  corrections that are not about your own data. Pick the form that fits; each
  asks for what we need. Issues are public, so leave out anything private.
- **A pull request** in this repository, for README fixes only.
- **Email** hello@galacticoutreach.com, if you would rather do none of these.

## Before you report

- Check the [changelog](https://galacticoutreach.com/changelog). It may already
  be fixed.
- Check the open issues, in case someone has reported it already. Add what you
  saw to theirs rather than opening another.
- Try it once more and note exactly what you did. A bug you can reproduce is a
  bug we can fix; one you cannot is a conversation.

## What makes a report useful

Three lines, in this order:

- **What you did.** The steps, in the order you took them.
- **What you expected.** Sometimes the bug is that the product is confusing,
  and this is the line that says so.
- **What happened instead.** The actual behaviour, and the exact text of any
  error.

A short description is usually enough. If it involves a specific campaign, a
template or a send, say which. We can look it up.

Screenshots help for anything visual. **Never post your lead list, API keys,
passwords or anyone's contact details**, in an issue or anywhere else. We can
find the record from your campaign without them.

## What happens to it

A report from the dashboard becomes a ticket you can follow on the Support
screen, shown as **Open**, **Fixed** or **Closed** with the date it last moved.
A GitHub issue is answered and labelled in the issue itself, and closed when it
is fixed or decided.

We do not promise a fixed turnaround for bugs and ideas. Security reports carry
a stated commitment: see [SECURITY.md](./SECURITY.md).

## What you agree to by contributing

Because the product is proprietary, contributions work one way:

- Anything you send (a report, an idea, wording, a README change) may be used
  to improve Galactic Outreach, freely and without any obligation or payment to
  you. You keep no rights over the product that result from it.
- You confirm that what you send is yours to give, and contains no one else's
  confidential or personal information.
- Sending something does not make you a party to the product's licence, and
  does not grant you any right to its source.

## What we will not do

- Accept code contributions: the source is private, so there is nothing
  public to change beyond the README.
- Take paid feature work through this repository. If you want something built,
  email us and say so directly.
